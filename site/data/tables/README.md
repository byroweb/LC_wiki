# Machine-readable tables (`site/data/tables/*.json`)

Machine-readable facts about the Lost City world, for tooling. Everything
is generated by `build/tables.py` from the Content checkout given to
`build/build.py --content <path>` (revision 254).
Every file starts with a `_generated` header (revision, content commit, date, what
the table covers and what it does not). Keys are the content's debugnames
(`bronze_axe`, `generalshopkeeper2`, `loc_1276`) exactly as in the content. Coordinates are absolute world tiles `{level, x, z}`
(`0_50_50_21_18` -> level 0, x 3221, z 3218). JSON is pretty-printed with sorted
keys, so diffs between content versions are readable.

Regenerate with `update.bat` (pulls the content, rebuilds everything, runs
`build/validate_tables.py`, which fails the build if any item/npc/loc/area
reference does not resolve) or `python build/build.py --content source/content --tables-only`.
Nothing in these files is typed from memory of the game; the generator raises when
the content lacks something it expects (tool names, for example, are read from the
`[opheldu,X]` handlers and `inv_total(inv, X)` checks of the skill scripts).

## areas.json
Named places with rectangles. Kinds: `label_place` / `label_town` / `label_region` /
`label_underground` are the map labels from `maps/labels.txt` (and their +6400
underground twins) with heuristic squares (±12 tiles for a place, ±40 for a town,
region = bounding box of its towns), `zone` rows are exact rectangles from
`coord_pair_table` db rows (Wilderness, mage arena, random-event zones, bank
zones...), `bank` areas are the bounding box of a bank's booths (+3), and `stand` /
`npc_stand` areas are the bounding boxes of the clusters in `resource_stands.json`,
named after the nearest town ("Logs west of Varrock (tree x15)", "Chicken x9 north
of Lumbridge"). `parent` links a place to its region and a stand or bank to its
nearest label; `regions` carries the wiki's region tags. Not covered: exact town
borders (the content has none) and indoor rooms.

## recipes.json
The skill recipe graph for woodcutting, mining, smelting, smithing, cooking,
fletching, firemaking, crafting, fishing and herblore: `product` (or `product_loc`
for a fire), `skill`, `level`, `inputs`, `tool` (plus `tools` with a per-tool level
for axes and pickaxes), `station` with `station_locs` (or `station_npcs` for fishing
spots) and `station_examples` (the three placements nearest the Lumbridge respawn),
`xp` in levels of experience (content values ÷ 10), `produces_n`, `source`, and
where they apply `byproducts` (the emptied pot a dough hands back) and `burnt` (what
a failed roll gives instead).

`skill` is the skill trained, or `none` for a step nothing trains -- picking a field,
pulling the legs off a toad, opening an oyster, joining the two crystal key halves --
and `magic` for a spell that turns one item into another (charging an orb on its
obelisk, enchanting a ring). A consumer needs the row either way. `inputs[].n` is
what ONE action consumes, for `produces_n` made: fifteen feathers and fifteen shafts
make fifteen headless arrows, and the tipped-arrow, dart and bolt rows follow the
same rule, because their handler takes min(tips, partner, max) and deletes that
many of both.

`driver` says how the player starts it, which `station` cannot: most recipes have no
station at all, and the empty field hides three quite different mechanisms. It is one
of `op_loc` (click the tree, rock or fishing-spot object), `op_npc` (click the NPC --
a fishing spot usually is one), `op_item` (click the item in your pack, as when
identifying a herb), `use_item_on` (use one item on another and it just happens --
bronze darts), `use_item_on_loc` (use an item on a wheel, fire, furnace or sand pit),
`chat_picker` (a menu of options appears -- arrow shafts) or `interface` (a full
interface opens -- leather gloves). A `chat_picker` row also carries `picker` with
the option `labels` and how many `choices` there are; those strings are the only
handle a caller has, since the icons beside them are live 3D models. Both vary by
row: cutting a log that yields shafts offers three fixed labels, and one that does
not offers two built from the log's name ("Oak Short Bow."). An input may carry
`per`, meaning n of it per that many products -- leather takes one reel of thread
per five items, and refuses to start without a reel in the pack. Read from
`trees.dbrow`, `mine.dbrow` + `pickaxe_checker.rs2`, `smelting.struct`,
`smithing.dbrow`/`.struct` (hammer from the anvil handler), `cooking_generic.dbrow`
(its `cantcookmessage_fire` / `cantcookmessage_range` fields decide the station:
`range` for oven-only food such as bread, cake, pizza and pies, `fire` for
fire-only food such as swamp paste, `range_or_fire` otherwise, and `neither` when
the content refuses both),
the fletching db rows plus the `[opheldu,...]` handlers that name the partner item
and the knife, the log params in `firemaking.obj`, the crafting db rows and structs,
the herb `[opheld1,...]` handlers and the `brew_potion` structs, and the fishing
spot scripts (`fish_roll(...)` calls with their level gates).

Not every recipe has a config row. A step the content writes out longhand as a
`[label,...]` -- headless arrows, the ogre arrow chain, cannonballs, the dragon
square shield, and the whole cooking assembly layer (dough, pie shells, pizzas,
cake mix, stews, kebab bowls, gnome dishes) -- is read straight out of the block:
the `inv_del` calls are the inputs, the `stat(<skill>) <` guard is the level, the
`stat_advance` is the xp, and an item the block only counts with `inv_total` is a
tool. Same for a spot that rolls its own catch table instead of calling
`~fish_roll` (the big net: mackerel, cod, bass, seaweed, oysters, caskets), for the
gem rock, whose mining row has no output because the script rolls `gem_rock_table`
(each gem is its own row with its weight in the notes), and for the gnome dishes and
cocktails, which count what went in four bits per ingredient and settle it with an
if-chain of exact counts when the dish is cooked or the shaker poured -- the bit
ranges pair each counter with its ingredient, so every stage from half-baked to
finished is a row (gnome spice is counted but never used up, and the notes say so).
Where a block reads an item out of a slot, the pairs come from the handlers that
jump to it, so each real combination is its own row.

The header's `unobtainable_inputs` lists every ingredient of a recipe here that no
shop, drop, recipe, service, chest, handout, minigame, spawn or quest script in this
revision provides; only cheat scripts or the raw cache dump mention them, so a plan
that needs one cannot be carried out. It is computed, not typed, so it shrinks as
sources are found.

Not covered: runecrafting (see the wiki's runecraft layer), spells that do not
convert an item, agility, quest-only recipes; wine fermenting, which runs on a timer
rather than a click; the second ingredient of a part-filled kebab bowl, which the
struct row does not name; and tanning and Aggie's dyes, which are NPC services with
no level or xp and so live in `item_sources.json` instead.

## item_sources.json
For every obtainable item, where it comes from: `shop` (vendor debugname, inventory,
stock, and `opens_by`: `op Trade` when an `opnpc3` handler exists for the vendor or
its category, `talk` when the Talk-to dialogue reaches `openshop`; both are listed
when both work), `drop` (NPC debugname, rate such as `1/128` when the death script
gives one, count, sub-table path), `skill` (recipe summary), `service` (an NPC that
turns items into another item for coins -- the three tanners and Aggie's dyes --
with `from`, how many `n` of it she takes and the price that NPC charges), `chest` (a
locked loc the `key` opens: `always` is true for loot given every time, false for one
roll -- the crystal chest always holds an uncut dragonstone), `handout` (an NPC that
hands the item over free on request: Aluft Gianne and the gnome cooking utensils, which
no shop stocks), `minigame` (a payout, with the `level` gate and `xp`: the Fishing
Trawler is the only source of sea turtle and manta ray), `spawn` (ground spawn
tiles from the map files) and `quest` (quest scripts that reference the item). Not
covered: pickpocket and stall loot (already in `thieving.gen.json`) and random-event
rewards (see `npc_safety.json`).

## npc_safety.json
Every NPC config: `attackable`, `aggressive` (true when its `.hunt` config hunts
players and switches to an attack mode; `null` when the hunt mode lives in the
engine), `hunt` details (`only_weaker` mirrors `check_nottoostrong`), `huntrange`,
`poison` (the `poison_severity` param), `maxHit` computed with the content's own
`npc_melee_maxhit` / `npc_ranged_maxhit` formula, `hitpoints`, `attackSpeed`,
`wanderRange` / `maxRange` (engine defaults 5 / 7 when unset; `maxRange` is `null`
for NPCs with no hitpoints and no Attack op, since they cannot be targeted, and for
`moverestrict=nomove` NPCs, which cannot chase), `moverestrict`,
the `areas` it spawns in, `placed`, false for a config nothing puts in the world,
`hunts` (aggressive AND huntrange > 0: a block can say attack while a range of 0 means
it never looks), and `wilderness_spawns`, how many of its spawns fall inside the
exact Wilderness rectangles (the `coord_pair_table` zone, carried in the file's
`wilderness` header along with the rule).

The `hunt` block names every check the engine applies when the NPC scans for a
target, read from its `.hunt` config: `level_check` is `outside_wilderness` when it
skips players over twice its vislevel -- the classic exemption, which does NOT apply
inside the Wilderness rectangles, where every hunter attacks any level -- or `off`
when it never skips; `tolerates` is true when `check_afk` is on (the engine default),
meaning it ignores a player who has stayed within one 21x21-tile zone for
`tolerance_ticks` ticks (1000 -- read from the engine's Player.updateAfkZones, ten
minutes; leaving the zone resets it), and false only for the bosses that set it off;
`keeps_hunting`, `line_of_sight`, and `needs_adjacent` (op-mode blocks walk up before
acting, melee reach; ap-mode fire from range). `guessed` is true when the content's own
comment above the block says it was reconstructed: the four standard modes
(`cowardly`, `ranged`, `aggressive_ranged`, and their kin in the unpacked cache dump)
carry "// guessing that ...", while `aggressive_melee` is marked official and the
bespoke blocks under areas/ are deliberate.
The unplaced ones are mostly leftovers in the unpacked cache dump rather than content;
filter on `placed` before ranking anything, which is what removes the two attackable
rows that have no hitpoints and so cannot be ranked at all. `random_events` lists the random-event NPCs
(`scripts/macro events/`) with flags read from their scripts: which skill folder
triggers them, whether they attack if ignored, teleport you, poison or damage,
whether Talk-to exists, what they say, and what they hand out. Not covered: magic
max hits.

## landmarks.json
Things to route to: `bank_booth` (tile, a stand tile on the side opposite the
nearest banker, `area` slug), `ladder` / `stairs` / `trapdoor` / `cave` / `passage`
with `op` and the destination tile (resolved from the `[oploc*]` scripts by
`build/portals.py`), every clickable `gate` and the `door`s whose script needs
coins, a quest variable, a lockpick or prints a "locked" message (`requires`),
`transport_npc` (NPCs whose dialogue moves you: boats, wizards, gliders; `to` tiles,
coin cost and quest variables read from their scripts; teleports to the respawn
point are ignored), and skill stations (`range`, `fire`, `furnace`, `anvil`,
`spinning_wheel`, `potters_wheel`, `pottery_oven`, `altar`; `use_item_on` is true
when the station has no click op). Not covered: shop doors (use the vendor spawn in
`shops.gen.json`), plain doors, and agility obstacles that do not teleport.

## resource_stands.json
Clusters of the same resource on the same floor: trees per log type and rocks per
ore (Chebyshev gap 4, so a wood stays a wood instead of chaining across a town),
fishing spots per fish (gap 6), with `centre`, `count`, `radius`, `bbox`, every
`tile`, the `refs` with a `ref_kind` saying whether they resolve against the loc or
the npc table (trees and rocks are locs, fishing spots are NPCs), `near` (nearest label and nearest town, each with a
compass direction and distance), `nearest_bank` (slug, name, distance, two stand
tiles) and `area` (its slug in `areas.json`). `npc_stands` does the same for
attackable NPC species (gap 8, at least two spawns) for training. Single trees are
kept as one-tree stands. Not covered: script-spawned resources; depleted rock and
stump stages count as the live loc.

## chat_verdicts.json
Every `mes(...)`, `~mesbox(...)` and `~objbox(...)` string in the scripts plus the
`displaymessage` enum, with occurrence counts and source files, classified by
regex into `cant_reach`, `inventory_full`, `no_coins`, `out_of_stock`,
`need_level`, `need_tool`, `locked`, `members`, `quest_required`, `busy`,
`nothing`, `danger`, `cant_do`, `progress` and `other`. `has_placeholders` marks
strings with `<...>` substitutions. Not covered: NPC speech (`npc_say`,
`~chatnpc`) and strings assembled entirely at runtime.
